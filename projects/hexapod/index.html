INDUSTRIAL PROJECT / DSDA · 2023–2024

6-DOF Industrial Motion Simulator

Stewart–Gough Platform · 2.2-Ton Payload · Industrial Motion Control

AXISXTREME is a high-capacity, six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) motion simulator based on a Stewart–Gough parallel mechanism, developed for advanced simulation, research, and motion-testing applications.

The platform uses six servo-driven linear actuators to reproduce coordinated translational and rotational movements—surge, sway, heave, roll, pitch, and yaw. Designed for a 2.2-ton payload and acceleration capabilities up to 1 g, the system integrates heavy-load actuation, precision motion control, and industrial automation.

01 / PROJECT CONTEXT

Project Overview & Technical Details

AXISXTREME is an industrial-scale hexapod motion platform designed to reproduce complex motion profiles under high payload and dynamic loading conditions. Its Stewart–Gough configuration uses six actuated legs connecting a fixed base to a moving platform, enabling motion in six degrees of freedom.

Developing such a system requires balancing payload weight, rotational inertia, center-of-mass location, workspace, and motion requirements against actuator force, velocity, and mechanical transmission limitations. The platform geometry must accommodate the desired displacement, velocity, and acceleration in each axis while supporting different operating scenarios.

The engineering development involved deriving the platform's kinematic and dynamic equations and using MATLAB and Simulink to model, simulate, and optimize the system. These analyses guided the selection of platform dimensions, ball-screw assemblies, transmission ratios, pulley and belt configurations, and servo motor specifications.

The control system was developed around a Siemens S7-1517 PLC and Lenze i950 servo drives. Motion sequences were evaluated in MATLAB before implementation in PLC control logic. Two operator-interface solutions were developed: a Siemens TIA Portal-based HMI for panel operation and a LabVIEW-based interface for PC-to-PLC communication and control.

The project integrates parallel-robot modeling, constrained mechanical optimization, electromechanical transmission design, PLC-based motion control, and operator-interface development into a high-capacity industrial automation system.

02 / PERSONAL CONTRIBUTIONS

My Role & Contributions

I contributed to the system-level engineering and control development of AXISXTREME, from mathematical modeling and mechanical sizing to actuator selection, PLC programming, HMI development, and physical integration.

Kinematic & Dynamic Modeling:

I derived the kinematic and dynamic equations of the Stewart–Gough platform and developed MATLAB models to evaluate its motion and loading characteristics. I also used Simulink to cross-check simulation results and assess the validity of the mathematical models.

System Sizing & Optimization:

I optimized the dimensions and geometry of the fixed base and moving platform based on payload mass, inertia, center-of-mass position, and required displacement, velocity, and acceleration in each axis. I evaluated motion scenarios while accounting for actuator force and velocity constraints and component availability.

Actuator & Transmission Selection:

Based on modeling results, I sized and selected ball-screw-and-nut mechanisms, transmission ratios, belt and pulley configurations, and servo motor requirements. The calculations considered actuator loads, travel, speed, motor torque, and power.

Motion Control & PLC Programming:

I developed and simulated motion-control strategies, including point-to-point movements, in MATLAB before translating them into PLC control logic using Structured Text and Ladder Logic. I worked with the Siemens S7-1517 PLC and Lenze i950 servo drives to implement coordinated motion control.

HMI Development & Communication:

I developed two operator-interface solutions: a TIA Portal-based HMI for controlling the platform through the PLC and a LabVIEW interface for PC-to-PLC communication, monitoring, and control.

Electrical Checks & Mechanical Assembly:

I assisted with electrical wiring checks and the mechanical assembly of the actuator systems, contributing to the verification of connections, component integration, and assembly quality.
