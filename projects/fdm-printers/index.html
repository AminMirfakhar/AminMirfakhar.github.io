FDM 3D Printers Cartesian & CoreXY Development

SolidWorks · Arduino Mega / RAMPS · Marlin Firmware

I designed and helped build a custom Cartesian FDM 3D printer, then independently redesigned and rebuilt it as a CoreXY system. The project covered SolidWorks CAD, linear motion design, electronics, wiring, Marlin firmware configuration, calibration, and test printing.

The CoreXY conversion reused much of the original hardware, with newly designed and 3D-printed components to accommodate the revised motion architecture.

01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW & TECHNICAL DETAILS

Project Overview & Technical Details

This academic engineering project at Iran University of Science and Technology involved the design, construction, and subsequent modification of a custom fused deposition modeling (FDM) 3D printer. The work progressed through two distinct stages: an initial collaboratively built Cartesian printer, followed by an independently executed CoreXY conversion.

Stage 1 — Cartesian Printer

I developed the printer's mechanical design in SolidWorks. The original layout used an aluminum-profile frame, linear guide rods and bearings, belt-driven X and Y motion, and lead screws for the Z axis. The electronics included an Arduino Mega with a RAMPS shield, stepper drivers, stepper motors, a power supply, and mechanical microswitch endstops.

The project team assembled and wired the machine, configured Marlin firmware for the custom hardware and motion arrangement, and performed calibration and test printing. The initial project proposal specified a target build volume of 400 × 400 × 400 mm and a target resolution of 100 µm; these were design objectives, not documented measured results.

Stage 2 — CoreXY Redesign

I later initiated a redesign of the original machine in SolidWorks to use CoreXY motion, in which coordinated belt movement from two motors controls planar positioning. The new design retained much of the existing mechanical and electronic hardware, while requiring a revised motion layout and newly designed, 3D-printed parts.

I carried out the CoreXY conversion independently, including the redesign, fabrication of additional parts, disassembly and reassembly, complete electrical wiring, firmware reconfiguration, calibration, troubleshooting, and test printing. The result was practical experience in taking an electromechanical prototype through two complete motion-system configurations.

02 / MY ROLE & CONTRIBUTIONS

My Role & Contributions

My responsibilities extended from CAD and component selection through physical assembly and commissioning. The first printer was a collaborative build; the CoreXY redesign and its mechanical, electrical, and firmware implementation were my independent work.

SolidWorks Mechanical Design

I designed the 3D assemblies for both machine configurations, developing the frame, linear-axis mechanisms, and component arrangements. I redesigned the mechanical layout for CoreXY while reusing existing hardware wherever practical.

Linear Motion & Transmission

I worked with guide rods, linear bearings, belt-driven X–Y axes, and lead-screw Z motion on the Cartesian printer, then redesigned the planar belt transmission around the CoreXY architecture.

Electronics Selection & Integration

I helped select and integrate the Arduino Mega, RAMPS controller, stepper motors and drivers, power supply, and endstop switches. For the CoreXY version, I completed the electrical integration and wiring myself.

Marlin Firmware Configuration

I configured Marlin for the printer's hardware and motion system, then independently updated its configuration for CoreXY kinematics, motor operation, and axis setup.

Custom 3D-Printed Components

I redesigned and printed additional mechanical parts to support the CoreXY conversion, focusing on using available components instead of replacing the entire printer.

Mechanical Assembly & Wiring

I participated in constructing and wiring the original Cartesian printer, then independently disassembled and rebuilt the system with its new mechanism and electrical layout.

Calibration, Testing & Troubleshooting

I calibrated axis motion and endstops, tested machine operation through 3D printing, and worked through mechanical and firmware issues during commissioning, including the independent CoreXY upgrade.
