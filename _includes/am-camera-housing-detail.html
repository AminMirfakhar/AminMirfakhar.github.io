INDUSTRIAL PROJECT / DSDA · 2024

Industrial Camera & Illumination Housing

Mechanical Design · Environmental Protection · Thermal Management

Designed and developed protective industrial camera and illumination housings for machine-vision applications in harsh mining and mineral-processing environments. The assemblies incorporated IP66-oriented environmental protection, dedicated air-cooling and water-cooling configurations, sealed optical windows, and adjustable mounting systems.

I was responsible for the complete mechanical design, from initial CAD development and component integration through manufacturing preparation, assembly, and installation support. The housings supported the PelletSense and Conveyor Belt Monitoring projects.

01 / PROJECT CONTEXT

Project Overview & Technical Details

Industrial machine-vision equipment operating in mining and mineral-processing facilities must withstand dust, moisture, elevated temperatures, and demanding installation conditions while maintaining reliable optical performance.

This project involved developing protective housings for industrial cameras and illumination equipment, combining environmental protection, thermal management, optical integration, and mechanical adjustability in manufacturable assemblies.

The camera and illumination housings incorporated sealing interfaces, gaskets, and protective optical windows to support an IP66-oriented design. Particular attention was given to maintaining enclosure integrity while allowing access to internal equipment and preserving the optical path required for image acquisition and illumination.

To address different operating conditions, separate air-cooled and water-cooled configurations were developed. These designs required consideration of cooling connections, internal component arrangements, available installation space, and the thermal requirements of the enclosed equipment.

The mechanical construction combined machined cylindrical tubes, laser-cut plates, welded assemblies, and CNC-machined components. Precision-machined end caps, threaded interfaces, and mounting holes enabled assembly, sealing, and integration of the optical and cooling components.

Adjustable mounting systems were also developed to allow camera and illumination positioning for different industrial installations. These arrangements provided flexibility in viewing angles and equipment orientation while maintaining mechanical stability.

The designs were developed in SolidWorks and visualized using KeyShot, with attention to dimensional tolerances, manufacturing processes, assembly sequences, and component accessibility. The resulting hardware supported the PelletSense and Conveyor Belt Monitoring systems.

02 / PERSONAL CONTRIBUTIONS

My Role & Contributions

I independently designed the complete camera and illumination housings and their adjustable mounting systems, taking responsibility for their mechanical development from concept through manufacturing and physical integration.

Complete Mechanical Design & CAD Development:

I designed the entire camera and illumination housing assemblies and their adjustable mounting mechanisms in SolidWorks. This included the enclosure geometry, internal component arrangement, mechanical interfaces, mounting configurations, and assembly details. I also created KeyShot renderings for design visualization and technical presentations.

Environmental Protection & Sealing Design:

I developed the housings with provisions for IP66-level dust and water protection, incorporating gaskets, sealing interfaces, and protective optical windows. The design required balancing environmental protection with the mechanical interfaces, optical access, and assembly requirements of the equipment.

Air & Water Cooling Configurations:

I developed both air-cooled and water-cooled housing configurations to accommodate different thermal and operating requirements. I considered cooling arrangements, connection interfaces, internal space constraints, and mechanical integration with the camera and illumination assemblies.

Optical & Industrial Camera Integration:

I integrated the mechanical requirements of industrial cameras, lenses, illumination equipment, and protective optical windows into the enclosure designs. This included accommodating camera dimensions, optical access, positioning, and alignment while protecting the equipment from the industrial environment.

Adjustable Mounting System Design:

I independently designed adjustable mounting assemblies that allowed control over camera and illumination positioning and orientation. These mechanisms provided installation flexibility for different equipment layouts and required fields of view.

Design for Manufacturing & Precision Components:

I developed the housing designs around a combination of machined tubes, laser-cut metal plates, welded structures, and CNC-machined components. I designed precision end caps, threaded connections, mounting holes, and assembly interfaces while considering manufacturing tolerances, component fit, sealing, and practical assembly.

Manufacturing Oversight & Hands-On Assembly:

I monitored the manufacturing process, reviewed component quality and fit, and participated in assembling the housings and mounting mechanisms. I also supported packaging, equipment integration, and installation at industrial facilities.
