INDUSTRIAL R&D PROJECT / DSDA

BeltSense Conveyor Belt Monitoring

Industrial Computer Vision · Defect Detection · Condition Monitoring

BeltSense is an industrial machine-vision system developed to monitor conveyor belt condition, detect surface damage, and support preventive maintenance in mining operations.

Developed for Golgohar Mining and Industrial Company, the project progressed from technology benchmarking and feasibility studies to computer-vision prototyping and industrial deployment. Laser triangulation was investigated in R&D; the final installed system used camera-based inspection without laser scanning.

01 / PROJECT CONTEXT

Project Overview & Technical Details

Conveyor belts are critical components of mining and bulk material-handling operations, where unexpected belt damage can cause costly production interruptions. Continuous condition monitoring helps identify developing defects and supports effective maintenance planning.

BeltSense was developed to inspect conveyor belt surfaces and monitor damage and splice conditions during operation. The project began with an investigation of existing belt-monitoring technologies, including industrial solutions from manufacturers such as ROXON. Different approaches were compared based on their capabilities, implementation requirements, and suitability for Golgohar's operating conditions.

During the R&D phase, two approaches were explored. The first used line-laser projection and optical triangulation to estimate belt surface geometry and investigate surface-profile measurements. The second used camera-based computer vision to identify visible defects and monitor belt splices.

An initial Python-based image-inspection prototype was developed using YOLO and OpenCV, with a Streamlit web interface for demonstrating detections. A separate laser-triangulation algorithm was developed and tested during feasibility evaluation.

After technical assessment, the final system installed at Golgohar adopted vision-based inspection without laser triangulation. Its camera hardware used an imaging and protective-enclosure architecture similar to PelletSense, with a different lens configuration suited to the conveyor's field of view and working distance.

The project illustrates the progression from technology research and experimental inspection methods to an industrial machine-vision system for conveyor condition monitoring.

02 / PERSONAL CONTRIBUTIONS

My Role & Contributions

I contributed to BeltSense through technology benchmarking, requirements analysis, laser-triangulation experiments, computer-vision prototyping, client coordination, and participation in the manufacturing process.

Technology Research & Benchmarking:

I researched established conveyor belt monitoring products, including ROXON solutions, and compared inspection technologies, measurement capabilities, installation requirements, and limitations to identify suitable methods for Golgohar's needs.

System Requirements & Inspection Concept:

I evaluated the client's operational needs and investigated alternative monitoring architectures. The feasibility study considered line-laser triangulation for surface geometry measurement alongside image-based inspection for visible defects and belt splice monitoring.

Laser Triangulation Algorithm Development — R&D:

I developed and tested a prototype algorithm using projected laser lines and optical triangulation to investigate conveyor belt surface profiling. This work was limited to R&D: laser triangulation was not part of the final deployed system.

Computer Vision Prototyping:

I developed a Python prototype using YOLO and OpenCV to investigate image-based belt defect detection and built a Streamlit interface to demonstrate the concept and visualize results.

Camera & Optical Configuration:

I evaluated working distance, camera placement, lens selection, and field-of-view requirements for conveyor belt inspection. The final camera-based configuration built on the imaging architecture used in PelletSense, with optics tailored to this application.

Technical Proposal & Client Coordination:

I helped develop the technical solution and proposal for Golgohar and worked with its engineering team to refine inspection requirements and evaluate how different approaches matched site needs.

Manufacturing & Hardware Integration:

I participated in manufacturing activities and preparation of the industrial imaging hardware, contributing to the project's transition from a prototype concept toward production. Related protective camera and illumination housings are detailed in the Camera & Illumination Housing case study.

R&D to Industrial Implementation:

I contributed from initial technical research through prototype evaluation and manufacturing. The installed BeltSense system used camera-based defect inspection, reflecting the selected practical approach rather than the experimental laser-triangulation configuration.
